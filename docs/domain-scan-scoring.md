# External Hygiene Score (v2)\n\nThe External Hygiene Score is a deterministic **0–100 configuration-hygiene summary** based only on completed, evidence-backed public observations. It is **not an exploitability score** and does not replace a penetration test or vulnerability assessment.\n\n## Why v2 replaced the previous model\n\nThe previous v1 engine used large coarse deductions such as −20 for unenforced HTTPS and −25 for unavailable TLS. That made a single observation dominate the score and could make otherwise healthy domains appear disproportionately weak.\n\nVersion 2 uses six weighted dimensions and smaller, evidence-specific deductions. The total possible deduction is exactly 100 points, so the score remains intuitive while partial weaknesses have proportional impact.\n\n## Dimension weights\n\n| Dimension | Maximum deduction |\n|---|---:|\n| TLS & Certificate | 20 |\n| HTTPS & Transport | 15 |\n| HTTP Security Headers | 20 |\n| DNS & Domain Integrity | 15 |\n| Email Security | 15 |\n| Internet Exposure | 15 |\n| **Total** | **100** |\n\nThe score is calculated as:\n\n`Score = clamp(100 - TotalDeducted, 0, 100)`\n\nEach dimension is independently capped at its maximum deduction.\n\n## TLS & Certificate — 20 points\n\nThe scanner uses observations that are actually available from the TLS handshake:\n\n- TLS service unavailable: **−5**\n- Certificate is not authorized by the client trust store: **−4**\n- Certificate expires within 7 days: **−3**\n- Certificate expires within 30 days: **−1**\n- Observed legacy protocol below TLS 1.2: **−4**\n- Observed certificate SAN does not contain the scanned hostname: **−4**\n\nUnavailable certificate fields do not produce deductions.\n\n## HTTPS & Transport — 15 points\n\n- HTTP does not redirect to HTTPS: **−5**\n- HSTS is reported missing: **−4**\n\nHSTS is intentionally scored here rather than in the HTTP-header dimension so it cannot be double-counted.\n\n## HTTP Security Headers — 20 points\n\nThe current implementation assigns different weights according to the significance of each observed missing control:\n\n- Content-Security-Policy: **−5**\n- X-Content-Type-Options: **−3**\n- X-Frame-Options/equivalent framing control: **−3**\n- Referrer-Policy: **−2**\n- Permissions-Policy: **−2**\n- Other explicitly reported missing security headers: **−1 each**, bounded by the dimension cap\n\nHSTS is excluded because it belongs to HTTPS & Transport.\n\n## DNS & Domain Integrity — 15 points\n\nThe current implementation can score only signals it actually collects:\n\n- DNSSEC not observed: **−4**\n- Only one authoritative nameserver observed: **−2**\n- No public A/AAAA address or CNAME observed: **−2**\n- Excessively large TXT surface (>20 records): **−1**\n\nCAA is **not** treated as missing when it is not collected. This is deliberate epistemic safety: unknown is not equivalent to absent.\n\n## Email Security — 15 points\n\nSPF and DMARC are evaluated using their observed records and policy values:\n\n### SPF\n- SPF missing: **−4**\n- SPF uses `+all`: **−4**\n- SPF uses `?all`: **−2**\n- SPF uses `~all`: **−1**\n- SPF with a strict `-all` policy: no deduction\n\n### DMARC\n- DMARC missing: **−4**\n- `p=none`: **−3**\n- `p=quarantine`: **−1**\n- `p=reject`: no deduction\n\nIf no MX record is observed, the scanner applies **−1** with an explanatory validation message rather than assuming email is required.\n\nDKIM is not penalized because the current scanner does not have a selector-independent DKIM observation.\n\n## Internet Exposure — 15 points\n\nPassive Shodan InternetDB data is used without active port scanning:\n\n- Potentially risky database/management ports: **−5**\n- One or more distinct CVE identifiers in passive exposure data: **−5**\n- Exposure signals spread across at least three public hosts: **−3**\n\nCVE identifiers are deduplicated before scoring. Exposure deductions are bounded by the 15-point dimension cap.\n\n## Epistemic safety\n\nA failed, pending, or incomplete category never produces a deduction.\n\nFor example, if a DNS provider times out, DASS does not convert that failure into “SPF missing” or “DNSSEC missing.” The scan can instead report reduced completeness or a warning.\n\n> **Failure to observe a control is not automatically evidence that the control is absent.**\n\n## Determinism and explainability\n\nEvery deduction contains a human-readable observation. The UI can therefore answer why a domain received its score without hiding the calculation behind an opaque model.\n\n`computeExposureScore()` delegates to `computeScoreBreakdown()`, so the displayed integer score and detailed audit cannot silently diverge.\n\n## Scoring version\n\nCurrent model:\n\n**`scoringVersion: 2`**\n\nHistorical scans should retain their original scoring version so that future rule changes do not silently rewrite historical scores.\n\n## Posture interpretation\n\n| Score | Classification |\n|---:|---|\n| 90–100 | Excellent |\n| 75–89 | Good |\n| 60–74 | Fair |\n| 40–59 | Needs Improvement |\n| 0–39 | Poor |\n\nThese labels describe **observable external configuration hygiene**, not guaranteed security or exploitability.\n\n## Important limitation\n\nThe scoring model can only evaluate signals that the current scanner actually collects. It intentionally does not fabricate deductions for CAA, DKIM, HSTS values, or other controls when their underlying data is unavailable.\n
